Campaigns

Groups of generated hostnames. A family is numbered siblings under one parent, such as {n}.stg.content-loader.com. A marker campaign is the same label across at least three different registered domains, such as 447.*.site. Dismissing a campaign hides matching hostnames from review until it expires. It does not allow or block DNS.

Thresholds

A domain must score at least the minimum and have a known registration date younger than the maximum age.

How scoring works

A hostname is queued only when its score reaches 3 and it has at least two independent signals, so a numbered name such as 0.pool.ntp.org is not enough on its own. Higher scores indicate more evidence, not certainty.

Signal sources

Shown as tags on each candidate.

Bifrost signal
Bifrost's own analysis of the name: random-looking labels, numbered subdomains, generic hyphenated words, unusual TLDs, depth and length.
cname to blocked
The hostname's answers alias (CNAME) to a name your blocklists or manual blocks cover: a tracker hidden behind a first-party name. Counts as two signals.
blocked sibling
Another hostname under the same parent is already blocked, so your Block decisions carry over to the rest of the family.
newly seen
First queried on your network within the last 7 days. Only applied once the query log holds 30 days of history.
widespread
Queried by many of your devices, which points to shared infrastructure such as NTP, OS updates or a CDN. Subtracts points.
single client no csp
Informational. Single client, permissive CORS and missing CSP are shown for context but add no points: they are true of most hosts.
Registry signal
RDAP facts such as registration age or pending deletion. A registration two or more years old (established) subtracts points. Looked up once per registrable domain and cached.
RDAP checked
Registry data was retrieved; anything adverse appears as a registry tag. RDAP queued means a lookup is waiting in the background. RDAP not checked means the domain has no lookup yet or its TLD has no known registry.
